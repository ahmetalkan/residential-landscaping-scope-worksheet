Residential Landscaping Scope Worksheet: Plan Grading, Water and Bids

Landscaping budgets run away fastest when plants go in before drainage, grading, utility marking, and hardscape borders are sorted out. Dividing a yard project into structural groundwork, irrigation, and softscaping lets you phase work cleanly without tearing up fresh sod later.

The U.S. EPA WaterSense program notes that outdoor irrigation accounts for a substantial portion of residential water use—much of it lost to poor zoning, overspray, or thirsty non-native turf—while the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map provides the baseline winter cold-tolerance guide for selecting perennial shrubs and trees that survive locally.

  1. Utility locate (811) and demolition: Mark buried gas, electric, water, and telecom lines before clearing stumps, old concrete, or invasive brush.
  2. Grading, drainage, and hardscape: Correct slopes away from the house and build walkways, patios, and walls while heavy machinery still has open access.
  3. Irrigation lines and low-voltage lighting conduit: Trench and pressure-test lines before topsoil leveling.
  4. Soil preparation, trees, shrubs, mulch, and turf: Install large canopy trees first, then shrubs/perennials, followed by mulch and sod or seed last.
Check local HOA architectural rules, municipal tree-removal permits, and property-line easements before removing mature trees or installing permanent fencing and walls.
