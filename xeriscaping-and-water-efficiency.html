Xeriscaping and EPA WaterSense Yard Principles

Water-smart landscaping does not require covering an entire front yard in barren rock. EPA WaterSense principles focus on grouping plants by moisture need, improving soil organic matter, and limiting high-water turfgrass to areas people actually walk and play on.

Practical water-saving levers

  • Hydrozoning: Keep drought-tolerant perennials on a separate irrigation zone from vegetables or lawn turf so dry-adapted roots are not overwatered.
  • Drip irrigation in planting beds: Delivers water straight to root zones with far less wind drift and evaporation than overhead pop-up spray heads.
  • Mulch coverage: A 2-to-3-inch layer of organic wood mulch cools soil, cuts evaporation, and suppresses weeds—keep mulch pulled a couple of inches back from tree trunks and house siding.
  • Check local turf-replacement rebates: Many municipal water utilities offer rebates when replacing unused lawn strips with climate-appropriate native beds.
