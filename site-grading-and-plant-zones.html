Site Grading, Utility Locates and USDA Plant Hardiness Zones

Plants rarely fail because of bad luck; they fail because a full-sun shrub was planted in deep afternoon shade, clay soil stayed waterlogged, or a species was chosen one winter zone too warm.

Four site checks before drawing a planting plan

  • Call 811 at least a few business days ahead: Free public utility marking identifies electric, gas, water, and fiber lines—remember that private lines (like detached-garage power or sprinkler pipes) still require private locating.
  • Check your USDA Hardiness Zone: Look up your ZIP code on the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map and account for sheltered microclimates or exposed windy hills.
  • Do a simple percolation check: Dig a 12-inch hole, fill it with water, let it drain, refill, and time how fast water drops—slow-draining clay needs raised beds, organic compost incorporation, or moisture-tolerant species.
  • Map mature plant size: Space shrubs for their five-year mature width so branches do not crowd siding, windows, or walkways.
